I'm showing another way to find items to sell: **starter equipment from fresh characters**. In the original method, I create a character, mail eligible gear to my main and list it.

I credit **Sharken** for the idea. Some buyers wanted the low-level look, and I found a gap between that demand and the gear people were listing.

## Check the market first

This is my **December 6, 2017** method. I report the best results with **Worgen and Draenei** gear at the time, and avoid Human and Dwarf gear because I found more supply.

Check the exact item, its binding and alternative sources today. [Blizzard's patch 10.0.5 notes](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23892227/updated-january-24-dragonflight-10-0-5-content-update-notes) say former starter equipment returned to vendors in **Stormwind and Orgrimmar**. That update also added common and poor-quality gear to transmogrification.

The old scarcity story isn't enough on its own. A buyer may have a cheap vendor option, and today's starting equipment may differ from my footage.

## The original create-and-mail method

1. Create a fresh character with starter items you've already checked.
2. Inspect the equipment and identify eligible tradeable pieces.
3. Unequip them and reach an accessible mailbox.
4. Make sure the character has money for postage.
5. Mail the eligible gear to your main.
6. Receive it and check the auction price before listing.

I initially forget the postage money in the video. I point out that you can send money to the new character so it can mail the gear back.

Mail access and starting-zone progress can vary. Check that the character can reach a mailbox and that the item can be sent before repeating.

## Test a few listings

I look at sale-rate information and don't focus on weapons with weak demand. Check the item rather than assuming everything a new character wears is worth listing.

In 2017 I describe sales around **2,000, 4,000, 6,000 and sometimes 10,000 gold**, and report selling ten or eleven items in a day. Those are historical creator reports, not current prices or an independently verified sales record.

Start small. If the item is plentiful or has a cheap vendor source, a large asking price doesn't prove there's a profitable market. Allow for auction costs and relisting.

## Check delivery before removing a character

The video deletes a disposable character after sending its items. You don't need to delete a character to test this method.

Keep it until you've checked the recipient and confirmed delivery. Don't copy that step onto an established character.
