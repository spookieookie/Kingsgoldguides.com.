I've got an **Obliterum shuffle** from Legion for you guys. I'm buying items off the auction house, breaking them down at the **Obliterum Forge in Dalaran**, and turning the ash into Obliterum to sell.

**This is an archive of the old Legion method.** The current Retail records for [Obliterum](https://www.wowhead.com/item=124125/obliterum) and [Obliterum Ash](https://www.wowhead.com/item=136342/obliterum-ash) show that these materials have lost their power. Don't buy supplies expecting this to work as a current Retail gold farm.

## What the shuffle does

The old process was pretty simple:

1. Buy eligible items for less than the value of the ash they would produce.
2. Destroy them at the Obliterum Forge.
3. Combine **100 Obliterum Ash into one Obliterum** under the original Legion system.
4. Sell the Obliterum and subtract what you spent.

I credit **BregVids** for the technique in the recording. The version I'm showing buys the inputs, so you don't need to craft those items yourself. You still need access to the forge and a game version where the system actually works.

## Start with the price of one Obliterum

I start by checking the auction house. In the video, I look at a **stack of ten Obliterum listed around 17,000 gold** and use a spreadsheet to compare input items.

Here's the important correction when following that example: **a stack price is not a unit price**. If ten Obliterum cost 17,000 gold, one is worth 1,700 gold before selling fees.

| Part of the example | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Price for ten Obliterum | 17,000 gold |
| Price for one Obliterum | 17,000 ÷ 10 = 1,700 gold |
| Output at the sheet's 130-ash average | 130 ÷ 100 = 1.3 Obliterum |
| Gross output value per input | 1.3 × 1,700 = 2,210 gold, before fees |

With those numbers, **22,100 gold would be a tenfold overestimate of the gross value**. Don't use the roughly 22,000-gold buying cutoff mentioned in the recording without correcting the units and subtracting selling costs.

These are the old example numbers, not current prices or a guaranteed ash yield.

## Compare the cost against the ash

The spreadsheet shown in the video has columns for the item, average ash, items needed, and maximum buying price. I look at ring enchants in the pricing section, along with cheaper inputs elsewhere on the sheet.

The idea is to find the input that gives you the most output for what you spend. A large ash number on its own isn't enough. Compare the purchase price against the value of that ash, using the price of **one** Obliterum.

Use this calculation for the original conversion:

**Gross output value = average ash ÷ 100 × unit sale price of Obliterum.**

Your buying limit needs to be below that value after allowing for auction fees and other costs. For a completed batch, use what actually sold, subtract the input purchases and selling costs, and keep unsold stock separate.

I refer to a Google Sheet in the recording, but the current video description doesn't provide a working spreadsheet link. The calculation above gives you the core comparison without relying on that missing file.

## Break down a small batch at the forge

The [Dalaran demonstration starts around 3:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7G4IuxP8SHs&t=201s). I take the purchased items to the forge and obliterate them one at a time.

The recording shows returns such as **118 and 123 ash**. Those are individual examples from the footage. They don't establish that every input gives 130 ash or that the old spreadsheet averages apply in another version.

Test an eligible item before buying a large batch. Check that the forge accepts it, record the ash you receive, and compare that with the price you paid. If either the conversion or the margin doesn't work, stop there.

## Convert the ash and check completed sales

Near the end of the video, I combine the ash into Obliterum and head back to the auction house to list it. I describe quick sales in that old market, but listing an item isn't the same as getting paid.

Count the Obliterum you actually sell, the amount received after fees, and the cost of the items you destroyed. Leftover ash and unsold Obliterum are still inventory.

## About the 250,000-gold title

The original video is titled **World Of Warcraft 250,000 Gold In One Hour**. That is the recording's headline. This walkthrough does not verify that hourly profit, and the stack-versus-unit correction above matters when checking its example.

The useful part to carry forward is the buying discipline: check the output's unit price, measure the conversion, and leave room for selling costs before spending gold.
